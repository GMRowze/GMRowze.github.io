_Archontic_ or _Cainite_ are terms employed by theologians, historians, and inquisitorial authorities to describe a broad family of Christian heresies. Although their doctrines differ considerably, Archontic sects generally reject the assumption that Creation is wholly good, transparent, and governed directly by God. Instead, they tend to describe the cosmos as fallen, concealed, imprisoned, corrupted, or administered by lesser powers standing between humanity and the divine.

In Rowzencraft, the most important factions from this sect are the [Iberian Archontics](#31-iberian-archontics) and the [Children of Friends of Abel](#32-friends-of-abel).

## 1. Historical Sects

* **Marcionites:** The Marcionites followed the teachings of Marcion of Sinope, who argued that the God of the Old Testament was not the same being revealed by Christ. According to their doctrine, the material world was the work of a lesser creator, while the true God existed beyond Creation itself. Many later Archontic traditions regarded Marcion as one of the earliest witnesses to the distinction between the visible world and the divine reality beyond it.

* **Messalians:** The Messalians believed that every human soul harbored a demonic presence inherited at birth. Neither baptism nor ecclesiastical authority could remove this corruption; only continual prayer and direct mystical experience could accomplish that task. Contemporary accounts frequently associate the sect with visions, ecstatic trances, and claims of perceiving entities hidden from ordinary sight.


* **Paulicians:** The Paulicians were a dualist movement active throughout Armenia and the Byzantine frontier. Although much of their literature has been lost, both supporters and opponents described them as guardians of unusual scriptural traditions and doctrines concerning the separation of the spiritual and material worlds. Most historians regard them as one of the principal ancestors of later Archontic movements.

* **Arians:** Unlike most Archontic sects, the Arians did not reject Creation or the authority of Scripture. Their controversy centered upon Christ, whom Arius described as the first and greatest of God's creations rather than an eternal equal of the Father. Later heterodox traditions often incorporated Arian concepts into more elaborate hierarchies of celestial powers.

* **Adamites:** The Adamites sought to restore the innocence that existed before the Fall of Adam. Many communities practiced ritual nudity and rejected social conventions they regarded as products of humanity's fallen condition. Later Archontic authors increasingly transformed Adam from a biblical ancestor into a witness of realities that existed before mankind's separation from the divine order.

* **Cainites:** the Cainites reversed one of the central moral narratives of Scripture by portraying Cain not as the first murderer but as a victim of a flawed cosmic order. Several hostile sources accused them of venerating other condemned biblical figures, including Esau and Judas Iscariot. Over time, the term Cainite became a general label for dangerous or subversive Archontic sects regardless of their actual beliefs.

* **Borborites:** The Borborites are among the most mysterious of the early gnostic movements. Nearly everything known about them derives from hostile sources, which describe secret rites, forbidden scriptures, and elaborate cosmologies populated by hostile celestial powers. Whether these accusations were accurate remains unknown.

* **Phibionites:** Often associated with the Borborites, the Phibionites taught that the visible world concealed deeper spiritual realities inaccessible through ordinary religious instruction. Surviving accounts emphasize themes of forgotten knowledge, celestial exile, and the recovery of a lost divine inheritance. Their scarcity of sources has made them a persistent subject of speculation.

* **Lucianists:** The Lucianists are known only from scattered and often contradictory references. Most accounts associate them with hidden interpretations of scripture, intermediary celestial powers, and doctrines concerning the gradual dissolution of individual identity. Some historians have questioned whether they were ever a single coherent movement.

* **Sethians:** The Sethians traced their spiritual lineage to Seth, whom they regarded as the ancestor of a hidden and sacred bloodline. Their cosmology described a universe governed by layers of celestial powers and hostile rulers known as Archons. Many concepts later associated with Archontic thought first appear in their most developed form within Sethian literature.

* **Brethren of the Hidden Spirit:** The Brethren of the Hidden Spirit combined elements of medieval mysticism with older gnostic traditions. They taught that individuality itself was a temporary condition and that salvation consisted in reintegration with a higher spiritual unity. Their critics accused them of advocating the dissolution of the self, while their followers described the process as the highest form of enlightenment.

## 2. Beliefs

Common Archontic doctrines include:

* That the material world was created by a lesser, corrupt, ignorant, or hostile power rather than directly by God Almighty, or that matter itself possesses an inherent defect predating Creation (rejection of ex-nihilo).
* That divine revelation has been concealed, altered, fragmented, or deliberately suppressed.
* That salvation is attained through hidden knowledge, spiritual awakening, or esoteric initiation rather than faith alone.
* That angels, saints, celestial rulers, or other intermediary powers exercise authority independent of, or in opposition to, God.
* That humanity has forgotten its true origin and condition.

The designation Archontic derives from the term archon ("ruler"), a title frequently applied within these traditions to the invisible powers believed to govern the heavens, the material world, or both. The term Cainite is often employed in a similar manner, particularly within older ecclesiastical literature, where it gradually became a generic label for dangerous or subversive heresies regardless of their actual relationship to the historical Cainites.

Despite their common classification, Archontic sects possess neither a unified theology nor a continuous institutional lineage. Some regard one another as custodians of fragments of the same forgotten truth. Others denounce rival sects as servants of the very powers they claim to oppose.

## 3. Known Factions in Rowzencraft

### 3.1. Iberian Archontics

The Iberian Archontics were the earliest known Archontic currents to appear in South America. Most scholars trace their arrival to the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, when sailors, New Christians, missionaries, adventurers, and other travelers carried heterodox doctrines from the Iberian world into the frontier regions of the continent.

The movement is most commonly associated with the figure known as the Mad Spaniard near Paraguay during the early seventeenth century. Contemporary descriptions portray him as a scholar, prophet, heretic, madman. Fragments attributed to his writings repeatedly refer to a hidden corruption within Creation, forgotten revelations older than the Church, and "the rulers behind the rulers" who govern the world unseen. Several colonial legends connect the Mad Spaniard to abandoned settlements, vanished expeditions, and unauthorized missions established deep within the interior.

By the eighteenth century the Iberian Archontics had largely disappeared as an identifiable movement. Whether they were extinguished, absorbed into other traditions, or simply driven underground remains a matter of debate.

### 3.2. Friends of Abel

The "Friends of Abel", also known as Abyssal-Cainites, emerged during the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries as a radical reinterpretation of older Cainite doctrines. Rejecting the Sethian genealogies accepted by most Archontic traditions, they taught that humanity descends exclusively from Cain and that the figure of Seth was introduced later to conceal mankind's true origins.

Unlike most Cainite sects, however, the Abyssal-Cainites incorporated elements drawn from numerous maritime and eldritch cults. Their writings frequently reinterpret ancient sea deities, abyssal entities, and pre-Christian aquatic traditions through a Cainite theological framework. As a result, many scholars regard the movement as occupying an unusual position between the Archontic and Eldritch traditions.

According to Abyssal-Cainite tradition, Abel's body was preserved beneath the waters of the Deluge, where his descendants endured in hidden kingdoms beneath the seas, rivers, and flooded caverns of the world. These so-called Children of Abel were regarded not as monsters or spirits but as an elder branch of humanity retaining qualities lost after the Fall and the Flood.

Over time, the distinction between the Children of Abel and the entities venerated by certain maritime cults became increasingly blurred. Some Abyssal-Cainite texts describe them as descendants of Abel. Others portray them as servants, emissaries, or manifestations of far older powers dwelling beneath the waters since before human history.
