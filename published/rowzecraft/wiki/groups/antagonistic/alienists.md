The Alienists emerged during the twentieth century as a loose intellectual movement centered on the search for extraterrestrial intelligence and the prospect of first contact with non-human civilizations.

Originally composed of astronomers, engineers, physicists, mathematicians, psychologists, and government researchers, Alienism developed alongside major advances in astronomy, radio communications, aerospace technology, and computing. Its adherents generally rejected supernatural, religious, and occult explanations for unusual phenomena, preferring to interpret them through a strictly material and scientific framework.

Early Alienist literature treated extraterrestrial intelligence as a legitimate scientific possibility. Over time, however, certain factions came to regard the existence of advanced non-human civilizations not merely as probable, but as the most important fact in the universe. Contact with such intelligences was increasingly viewed as both inevitable and desirable, leading some groups to devote extraordinary resources toward its pursuit.

## 1. Beliefs

Although Alienism possesses no central doctrine or governing authority, most Alienist organizations share the conviction that humanity occupies an isolated position within a much larger cosmic environment populated by intelligences vastly older and more advanced than itself.

Alienist thinkers generally argue that such civilizations would possess scientific, technological, and philosophical knowledge far beyond anything presently available to humanity. As a consequence, Contact is often regarded not merely as a scientific discovery but as a civilizational event capable of transforming every field of human activity.

This assumption has produced significant disagreement within the movement. Some organizations advocate passive observation and the collection of astronomical data, while others support active attempts at communication through radio transmission, signal broadcasting, and the development of hypothetical contact protocols.

### 2.1. Groups in Brazil

Alienist activity in Brazil is primarily concentrated within academic, scientific, and technical circles. Unlike many other cultic movements, Alienist organizations rarely present themselves as religious or ideological groups and often operate under the guise of research institutes, observatories, amateur astronomy associations, and communications projects.

Several groups have historically maintained connections to universities and research centers in Campinas, São José dos Campos, and Rio de Janeiro. Smaller organizations are known to operate isolated radio observatories and listening stations throughout the interior of the country, particularly in sparsely populated regions of the Amazon and Central-West.

Although most such organizations are regarded as harmless scientific associations, a number have attracted controversy due to their secrecy, unusual funding sources, and apparent willingness to dedicate substantial resources to projects whose stated objectives remain unclear.
