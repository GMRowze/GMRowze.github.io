The Neopagans are a diverse collection of movements dedicated to the revival, reconstruction, or reinterpretation of pre-Christian religious traditions. Unlike the Archontics, who seek hidden truths behind established doctrine, or the Elder Cults, who pursue communion with primordial powers, the Neopagans generally look to the past, believing that ancient traditions preserve forms of knowledge, social organization, and spirituality lost during the Christian era.

Although many Neopagan groups are primarily cultural or religious in character, others have developed strongly political and militant tendencies. Such movements frequently emerge during periods of social upheaval, national crisis, or state collapse, when established institutions appear unable to provide meaning, identity, or legitimacy.

## 1. Known Factions

### 1.1. Followers of Perun

The Followers of Perun emerged during the 19th century and continued with the chaos of the Russian Civil War and the turbulent years that followed. Drawing upon Slavic folklore, nationalist mythology, and fragmentary pre-Christian traditions, they regarded Perun not merely as an ancient god but as a symbol of strength, struggle, and renewal through conflict.

Although never a unified organization, numerous armed bands, political societies, and occult circles adopted Perunite imagery during the 1920s. Several factions developed a particular fascination with modern warfare, interpreting industrial conflict as a continuation of ancient divine struggle. This tendency brought them into occasional contact with military figures, most notably Marshal Mikhail Tukhachevsky, whose interest in unconventional theories of warfare later attracted considerable speculation among both historians and occult investigators.

Most Perunite organizations disappeared during the political purges and consolidations of the 1930s. Nevertheless, rumors of surviving circles persisted throughout Eastern Europe, particularly among military, nationalist, and esoteric groups influenced by Slavic revivalist thought.
